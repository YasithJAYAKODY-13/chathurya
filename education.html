Her research

Bringing spacecraft home safely

The most dangerous minutes of any space mission are the last ones. Here is the problem her research works on, in four pictures.

  1. Step 1

    In orbit

    A spacecraft circles Earth at about 28,000 km/h.

  2. Step 2

    Hitting the air

    Coming home, it slams into the atmosphere at around 25 times the speed of sound. The air in front glows.

  3. Step 3

    Over 1,500°C

    The heat shield faces temperatures hotter than molten lava, while the inside must stay safe.

  4. Step 4

    Her work

    Chathurya simulates the airflow (CFD) and the heat moving through the structure (FEA) to find materials that survive.

Research paper, under peer reviewThe intense heat faced by spacecraft returning from space to Earth at very high speed

Research areas

  • Ultra-high-temperature alloys for space re-entry vehicles
  • Computational fluid dynamics (CFD)
  • Heat-dissipation analysis integrated with finite element analysis (FEA)

Why her work matters to everyone

  • Safer journeys home

    Better heat shields protect astronauts and spacecraft on the most dangerous part of the trip.

  • Cheaper access to space

    Spacecraft that survive re-entry can be reused, which lowers the cost of launching the satellites behind GPS, weather forecasts, internet and disaster warnings.

  • Everyday engineering

    The same heat-resistant materials and simulation methods help make jet engines, power stations and vehicles more efficient, using less fuel.
